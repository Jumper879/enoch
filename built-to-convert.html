the problems i fix

If your customers have to ask, your website can probably do it.

Every question you keep answering in DMs is a sign your website isn’t doing its job. Here’s what I build in its place, and what the research says about it.

“How much is it?”
→

Put your services and pricing in one place.

No more sending the same price list to every new enquiry. Customers see what you offer and what to expect before they ever message you.

34% of consumers say outdated or inaccurate information is the most frustrating thing on a business website. DreamHost, 2026

“Are you available?”
→

Give customers a booking and availability flow.

Let people see when you’re free and request a slot at 11pm on a Sunday, without waiting for you to reply.

Nearly 70% of consumers said they’d choose to book online when it’s offered. GetApp survey, 2021 (217 respondents)

“What products do you have?”
→

Build a proper product catalog.

Every product with photos, details, prices and reviews in one place, ready to browse at any hour.

270% higher purchase likelihood for a product with 5 reviews versus none, in a study of online retailers. Northwestern Spiegel Research Center

“How do I book?”
→

Give them a clear path from discovery to booking.

One obvious next step on every page, and a checkout or booking form with no unnecessary friction.

35.26% conversion gain is available to the average large e-commerce site from better checkout design alone. Baymard Institute

“Can I trust you?”
→

Put your work, testimonials, process and proof where customers can see them.

Show the real thing: your projects, your results, how you work and who you’ve helped.

60% of consumers say an outdated-looking website hurts a business’s credibility. DreamHost, 2026

“I want more information.”
→

Capture the lead instead of losing them in a DM.

A simple form sends every enquiry, with the details you need, straight to your inbox, ready for a quick reply.

Businesses that contacted a lead within an hour were nearly 7× as likely to qualify them as those who waited just one more hour (1.25M leads studied). Harvard Business Review

how it works

I don’t start with the homepage.

I start with your customers, and design backwards from what they need to do.

  1. 01

    Understand

    What is the business trying to achieve?

    Your goals, your customers and what a “win” looks like: more inquiries, more bookings, more sales.

  2. 02

    Identify

    Where are customers currently getting stuck?

    The questions people keep asking, the drop-off points and the trust gaps on your current site or social pages.

  3. 03

    Structure

    What information and actions should the website prioritise?

    The pages, content order and calls to action, so every section has a reason to exist.

  4. 04

    Design

    Build the visual experience around the customer journey.

    A look that feels like your brand and guides people from “what is this?” to “I’m in.”

  5. 05

    Launch

    Develop, test and deploy the website.

    Built, tested on real devices, then launched on accounts you own, with support after go-live.
